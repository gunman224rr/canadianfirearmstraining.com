What you'll actually learn

Content and testing are set by the RCMP. The course is built for absolute beginners — you do not need to have touched a firearm before, and you will not fire one here. All training uses non-firing firearms.

In the classroom

A full day of handling, law and safe practice, then the written and practical tests.

  • Major types of firearms, their parts and actions
  • Basic firearm safety and the ACTS/PROVE routine
  • Ammunition types and how to match them
  • Operating firearm actions
  • Safe handling and carrying procedures
  • Firing techniques and procedures
  • Care and cleaning of firearms
  • Responsibilities of an owner and user
  • Safe storage, display and transportation

Who takes it

The first step for target shooters and hunters, and a requirement for work in law enforcement, forestry and the film industry. It is also the right course if you have inherited a firearm and want to handle it properly.

  • You must be 18 to hold a full PAL or RPAL
  • Mature 12-year-olds may take the class for a Junior PAL
  • A Junior PAL converts to a full PAL at 18
  • Restricted always requires non-restricted first

Hunting in BC: the CORE exam

To hunt in British Columbia you must pass CORE — Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education. As of 2024 we no longer teach the course, but Murray still examines.

  1. Pick up a CORE manual in the gunroom at Island Outfitters and study it. Do the review questions at the end of every chapter.
  2. Email coreexaminer@shaw.ca to arrange sitting the examination.
  3. Pass the written examination and the firearms practical examination.
  4. Submit your paperwork and receive your certificate from the BC Wildlife Federation.
  5. Take that certificate and proof of BC residency to a Service BC office for your Fish and Wildlife ID, which you use to buy hunting licences and species tags.

Find an experienced hunter to mentor your first trips out. It will improve your success in everything you do outdoors.

Don't be afraid of firearms. Be cautious, practise safe handling, and you will live long and prosper.

Murray Kilshaw — instructor & CORE examiner

Questions we get every weekend

Where do I register?

In the gunroom at Island Outfitters, 1681A Island Highway, across from the casino. Registration and payment are in person.

How much does it cost?

The non-restricted PAL course is $175 and the restricted course is $100, payable in advance in the gunroom.

Where is the classroom?

At Island Outfitters, 1681A Island Highway, Victoria BC.

How long are the classes?

Both are full days. The PAL class starts Saturday at 8:45 AM and the restricted class starts Sunday at 10:00 AM.

Should I bring lunch?

If you like. There is a Tim Hortons and a Subway nearby that you can walk to on the lunch break.

How old do I have to be?

You must be 18 to obtain a restricted or non-restricted PAL. Mature 12-year-olds may take the class to obtain a Junior PAL, which converts to a full PAL when they turn 18.

Can I get my restricted PAL without the unrestricted one?

No. Anyone wanting a restricted PAL must have a non-restricted PAL first. This is government policy and we make no exceptions.

What if I already have my unrestricted PAL?

Then you can come Sunday for the restricted course on its own.

How long until my licence arrives?

Attach a passport photo and the required references to a properly completed application. Processing usually begins within 30 days of the RCMP receiving your documents, and most students have their PAL or RPAL 8–10 weeks after applying.

For questions about your application, call the Canadian Firearms Program at 1-800-731-4000. Be polite — the staff are knowledgeable and helpful if you let them be.

Is there any live fire in the course?

No. You will be trained with non-firing firearms.

How often is the course taught?

Twice a month on weekends. Ask at Island Outfitters for the current schedule.
